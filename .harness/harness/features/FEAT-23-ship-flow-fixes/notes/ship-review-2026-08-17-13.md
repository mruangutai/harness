## The one real miss, and why it matters more than the fix

SC-05 required each of the four angles present "in both their plan-surface and code-surface forms". Three angles carried the pair. **ALTITUDE carried neither** — and every gate said the criterion was met.

The cause: T-02's `verify:` grepped the two literals **file-globally**, so three conforming angles satisfied a clause that quantifies over four. qa then graded the same criterion met using the same file-global method. Two tiers made one mistake twice.

**Agreement between two readers using the same method is one measurement counted twice, not corroboration.** What found it was the goal-check reopening the file and counting per section. The transferable rule — a distributive clause needs a per-item check — is row B-20.

Fixed at `2cba9fb`; all four angles now measure 1/1. This was the feature's only cycle: `cycles_used` 4 of 10, and it sat at 3 for the entire build with no task ever routed back.

## What it cost, honestly

**A harness defect drove most of the waste.** `validate-digest.py --hook` fires on a lead's turn-end while its dispatched member is still in flight. A lead has no wait primitive, so its only options are a premature verdict or a fabrication. It fired **eight times**. It does not merely produce false returns — it manufactures a disk state (a `BLOCKED` digest beside unexplained new files) that reads exactly like an abandoned run.

**I misread one and dispatched T-05 twice.** One lead run and two member spawns, ~146k tokens, zero lines of code. The file that would have stopped me is the run's own `state.yaml`, which records `dispatched_at`/`completed_at` per step, and I never opened it. Nothing was corrupted — but not because the runner caught it. `mutates_repo: true` serialises within a run and is blind across runs. What caught it was one sentence I had put in both dispatches: *a verify that passes on an untouched tree is a finding*. The second member hit it, refused to overwrite the authentic receipt, and touched nothing.

**A mitigation now works.** Leads that hold their turn open with read-only calls until members return have defeated the hook **seven consecutive times**. It is a workaround on a DEC-174 surface only you can change (row B-12).

**Run count: 20 of a 20-run budget — at the bound.** That is informational, never a stop. My read: the runs earned their place with two exceptions — the duplicate T-05 run, and a correction round I caused by telling the validator lead its reviewers were write-less when `check-domain` grants each its own file. Both were my errors, not the org's. The twentieth run — your final validator pass — is the one that most clearly earned its place: it is the only reader that probed the argument class that broke `board-station.py`, and it caught three record defects nothing mechanical can see. The budget is informational and never stops a feature; I am reporting the number, not apologising for it.
